5 invisible forces that can flip a plane. On 7 Nov, jet blast from a departing airliner flipped a Cessna 172 onto its roof at John Wayne Airport (the instructor and student were unhurt; the FAA is investigating). Examiners love asking about these.

1. Airliner beacon flashing red: its engines are running. Blast from big jets has overturned small planes taxiing behind them (AIM 4-3-24).
2. Taxiing behind a jet powering up: stop and keep your distance, or ask ground for another route. Jet blast and prop wash can upset you at close range (AIM 4-3-18).
3. Helicopter hovering near your taxiway: stay at least 3 rotor diameters away from its downwash (AIM 7-4-7).
4. Taking off behind an airliner: rotate before its rotation point and stay above its climb path (AIM 7-4-6).
5. A light 1 to 5 knot crosswind can hold a wake vortex in the touchdown zone. A light quartering tailwind needs maximum caution (AIM 7-4-4).
