5 airport secrets hidden in plain sight. You walk past these every trip. Pilots read them like a map.

1. Runway numbers are the compass direction, minus the last zero. Runway 29 points about 290 degrees, and the other end is 11 (AIM 2-3-3).
2. O'Hare is ORD because it used to be Orchard Field. Chicago renamed it in 1949 for Navy hero Butch O'Hare, and the old code stuck.
3. A beacon flashing white and green at night marks a lighted land airport. Military fields add two quick white flashes (AIM 2-1-9).
4. The red and white lights beside the runway are PAPI lights. Two white and two red means on the glide path, all white is too high, all red is too low (AIM 2-1-2).
5. Newark is EWR because US airport codes can't start with N, which is reserved for the Navy. W and K are avoided too, since US radio stations use them.
