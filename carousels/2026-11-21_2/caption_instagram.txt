5 steps to a pilot license, in order. From first lesson to checkride, this is the path every student follows.

1. Get your paperwork done: a free FAA student pilot certificate and a medical certificate. You need both, and to be 16, before you solo (14 CFR 61.83, 61.23).
2. Fly your first solo: your instructor gives you a pre-solo knowledge test and a logbook endorsement first (14 CFR 61.87).
3. Fly solo cross-country: 5 hours in total, including one trip of 150 nautical miles with landings at three airports and one leg over 50 nautical miles (14 CFR 61.109(a)(5)).
4. Pass the written test: the FAA Private Pilot Airplane knowledge test is 60 questions in 2 hours, 70% to pass, and a pass stays valid for 24 calendar months (14 CFR 61.39).
5. Take your checkride: an oral exam and a flight with an examiner, graded against the FAA Airman Certification Standards. Part 61 needs at least 40 flight hours first (14 CFR 61.109).
