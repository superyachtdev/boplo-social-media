178 seconds inside a cloud. In the 1954 University of Illinois "180-degree turn" study, 20 pilots with no instrument training all lost control in simulated clouds, after 178 seconds on average. Would you get out?

1. Everything goes white: accept that it's an emergency and fly the attitude indicator with small, gentle inputs (FAA Airplane Flying Handbook, ch. 18).
2. Feel like you're turning? Your inner ear can't sense a slow turn and can fool you when you level out. Believe the instruments (FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, ch. 17).
3. Which way out? A gentle 180 degree turn back to the clear air you just left, with a shallow bank (Airplane Flying Handbook, ch. 18).
4. Call for help: ATC can give headings to clear air or the nearest airport. Squawk 7700 if needed (AIM 6-1-2, 6-2-2).
5. Why students train in a hood: private pilots need 3 hours of flight training by instruments alone (14 CFR 61.109(a)(3)). After training, the 1954 pilots escaped 59 times in 60.
