5 things pilots do when turbulence hits. A JetBlue flight from Puerto Rico hit severe turbulence near Florida on 17 November and 5 people were checked by medics after landing. Here is what examiners expect you to know.

1. Slow to maneuvering speed. Below it, the wing stalls before the airframe gets overstressed, and that speed drops when you fly light (Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, ch. 5).
2. Hold the attitude, not the altitude. Keep the wings level and the pitch steady, let altitude and airspeed swing, and tell ATC (AIM 7-1-27).
3. Stay at least 20 miles from a severe thunderstorm. Severe turbulence can reach that far, even in clear air, and radar shows rain, not turbulence (AIM 7-1-26, 7-1-27).
4. Know what "severe" means: large, abrupt changes in altitude or attitude, the aircraft may be momentarily out of control, and people are forced violently against their belts (AIM 7-1-21).
5. File a PIREP: location, time, intensity, altitude and aircraft type. Your report warns the next pilot (AIM 7-1-18).
