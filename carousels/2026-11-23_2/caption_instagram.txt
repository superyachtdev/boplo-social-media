5 things nobody tells you about your first flight lesson. Save this before you book.

1. You don't need a medical to fly with an instructor. You need it before you fly solo (14 CFR 61.23(a)(3)). Get it early anyway: finding a problem after 20 paid hours is the expensive way.
2. Your first flight can count. Fly with a certificated instructor and ask them to log and sign it (14 CFR 61.51). That time counts toward your license.
3. You will probably fly the plane. Many instructors hand you the controls within minutes, sometimes for the takeoff, while they stay ready on their own set.
4. The school asks your weight for a reason. Two adults, bags and full fuel can push a small two-seat trainer past its max weight (Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, ch. 10).
5. Feeling queasy is normal. Open the air vents, look outside at the horizon and tell your instructor early (Pilot's Handbook, ch. 17). It usually fades as you build hours.
