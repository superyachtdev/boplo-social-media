5 checkride questions that sound easy. Examiners love them because students rush the answer.

1. Why does the plane want to turn left? Four forces, not one: torque, spiraling slipstream, P-factor and gyroscopic precession. Right rudder fixes them (Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, ch. 5).
2. Can carb ice form on a hot day? Yes. Fuel vaporizing in the carburetor chills the air. Ice is most likely below 70°F (21°C) in humid air, but it can form at up to 100°F (38°C) (Pilot's Handbook, ch. 7).
3. 60° bank: what happens to stall speed? A level 60° turn pulls 2 G, and stall speed grows with the square root of load factor, so it rises about 41%. A 50-knot stall becomes about 71 knots (Pilot's Handbook, ch. 5).
4. Turning off north, what does the compass do? In the Northern Hemisphere it lags on turns from north and leads on turns from south. Remember UNOS: undershoot north, overshoot south (Pilot's Handbook, ch. 8).
5. Hot day, high airport, what changes? High density altitude means thin air: less engine power, less thrust and less lift. Expect a longer takeoff roll and a weaker climb (Pilot's Handbook, ch. 11).
