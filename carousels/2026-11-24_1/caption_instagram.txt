5 reasons your flight never flies straight. That curvy line on the seatback map isn't a mistake.

1. The curve is the shortest path. Flat maps stretch a round Earth, so a great circle route (like the arc over Greenland) looks bent on screen but is the shortest way between two cities on a globe.
2. Flying east is faster thanks to the jet stream, which often blows over 100 mph. North Atlantic routes are redrawn every day to ride it eastbound and dodge it westbound.
3. Twin jets stay near land for a reason. Without ETOPS approval, a twin-engine airliner must stay within 60 minutes of an adequate airport at one-engine-inoperative cruise speed (14 CFR 121.161).
4. Some skies are closed. Since 2022, Russia has banned most US and European airlines from its airspace, so many Europe to Asia flights detour, some by hours.
5. Planes follow highways in the sky. Flights use published airways between waypoints (AIM 5-3-4), and ATC adds turns for traffic and storms. Direct routing is a bonus, not the default.
