5 flight school differences nobody explains. There are two kinds of flight school, Part 61 and Part 141, and most beginners never ask which one they're joining. Save this before you sign up.

1. Same license at the end. Part 141 schools are FAA-certificated and follow an FAA-approved course. Part 61 training is flexible and built around you.
2. Fewer hours, on paper. The private pilot minimum is 35 flight hours at a 141 school (Part 141 Appendix B) vs 40 under Part 61 (14 CFR 61.109). Most students need more than either, so don't choose on this alone.
3. Built-in stage checks. Part 141 students must pass stage checks and end-of-course tests along the way. Under Part 61, your instructor decides when you're ready.
4. Some schools can give your checkride. A 141 school with at least a 90% first-time pass rate can earn examining authority (14 CFR 141.63), and its graduates can skip the separate FAA practical test (14 CFR 141.67).
5. Student visa or GI Bill? US student visas for flight training (SEVP) need a Part 141 or 142 school. VA flight training benefits at a flight school also need a Part 141 school or 142 training center, and you must already hold a private pilot license.
