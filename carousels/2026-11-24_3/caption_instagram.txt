5 moves when you don't know what to do. During this month's nationwide FAA ground stop, a student pilot on his first solo cross-country told ATC exactly that, then landed safely. Would you know your next move?

1. Keep flying the plane. Aviate, navigate, communicate: hold heading, altitude and airspeed first. A stable plane buys you time to solve everything else.
2. Say "student pilot" on first contact. The FAA asks you to (AIM 4-2-4), so controllers can give you extra help and patience.
3. Didn't understand ATC? Ask, never guess. If you're unsure of a clearance, request clarification right away (14 CFR 91.123(a)).
4. Have a plan B airport. Know a backup airport on every leg before you take off. When plans change mid-air, you turn toward it instead of starting from zero.
5. Lost or still stuck? Climb for radio and radar range, call any frequency on your chart or 121.5, and declare an emergency early if fuel runs low (Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, ch. 16; 14 CFR 91.3(b)).
