5 night flying questions examiners love. Sunset comes early this time of year. Number 1 has three right answers.

1. When does night start? Logging night: end of evening civil twilight (14 CFR 1.1). Night passenger currency: 1 hour after sunset (61.57(b)). Position lights on: sunset (91.209).
2. Why look beside a light, not at it? The center of your eye has no rods, the cells that see in dim light. Look 5 to 10 degrees off-center (PHAK Ch. 17).
3. How long for your eyes to adjust? About 30 minutes, and one bright light can undo it. Keep cockpit lights dim.
4. Why does a dark approach feel high? The black hole illusion: over water or unlit land, pilots feel higher than they are and descend too low. Trust the glidepath lights (AIM 8-1-5).
5. What do wingtip lights tell you? Red is left, green is right, white is the tail. See red and green together? It's heading straight at you (AIM 4-3-23).
