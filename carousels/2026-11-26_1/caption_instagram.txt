5 things a flight does to your body. Flying home for Thanksgiving? Here's what's happening to you at 35,000 ft.

1. The cabin is up to 8,000 ft, not sea level. Jets only have to keep cabin altitude at or below 8,000 ft (14 CFR 25.841).
2. Your blood oxygen drops by about 4 points. In a 502-person chamber study, saturation fell up to 4.4 points at 8,000 ft (NEJM 2007).
3. Food tastes bland. In a simulated cabin, salt tasted 20 to 30% weaker and sugar 15 to 20% weaker (Fraunhofer study for Lufthansa). That's why tomato juice tastes better up there.
4. Cabin air is desert dry. Humidity is usually below 20%, versus 30%+ at home. It dries eyes, nose and skin, but doesn't dehydrate your body (WHO).
5. Gas inside you expands by about a third at cabin pressure (Boyle's law). Same reason your ears pop: swallow or yawn on the way down.
