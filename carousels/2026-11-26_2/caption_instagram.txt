5 truths about learning to fly in winter. Starting lessons this season? Read this first.

1. Cold air helps. Dense air means more lift and more engine power, so takeoffs get shorter and climbs stronger (FAA PHAK ch. 11, density altitude).
2. Engines get preheated. Batteries weaken and oil thickens in the cold, so the FAA calls preheat advisable. Follow the maker's procedure (FAA Winter Flying Tips, P-8740-24).
3. Carry a CO detector. Many small planes heat the cabin with air warmed around the muffler, and a crack can leak carbon monoxide you can't smell (FAA Winter Flying Tips, PHAK ch. 17).
4. No solo after sunset without night training. Students need night training at that airport plus an instructor endorsement first (14 CFR 61.87(o)).
5. Pack a survival kit. The FAA suggests warm clothes and a survival kit on winter flights. Dress for the field, not just the heated cockpit.
